Simpson's paradox is a well-documented phenomenon in probability and statistics in which a trend appears in several groups of data but disappears or reverses when the groups are combined.
This result is often encountered in social-science and medical-science statistics and is particularly problematic when frequency data is unduly given causal interpretations.
The paradox can be resolved when confounding variables and causal relations are appropriately addressed and controlled in the statistical model.
In statistical inference and data visualization, ignoring subgroup distributions or treating aggregate visualizations as ground truth can lead to misleading conclusions.
Simpson's paradox illustrates that statistical association does not imply causation, and stratified analysis by relevant subgroups is critical when investigating complex empirical datasets.
When visual encoding is used as an exploratory tool rather than merely a presentation layer, it helps uncover hidden confounding factors and conditional relationships across data partitions.

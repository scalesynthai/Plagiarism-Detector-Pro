In diamond valuation and pricing models, the classical 4Cs—carat, cut, color, and clarity—serve as foundational attributes for determining market value.
Empirical pricing analyses often show an apparent paradox: lower-grade diamond colors or lower clarity ratings can appear to have higher median market prices in aggregate distributions.
This counterintuitive trend emerges because larger carat diamonds with lower clarity grades dominate the aggregate sample, masking the true marginal premium of high-clarity stones.
Python data analytics workflows leverage libraries like Pandas, NumPy, Seaborn, and Matplotlib to stratify pricing distributions across carat weight bins.
Detecting Simpson's paradox in diamond pricing demonstrates how exploratory data analysis and inferential visualizations expose confounding variable interactions that naive regressions overlook.
Applying log transformations and multi-variable regression reveals the true conditional price elasticity with respect to cut precision and clarity standards.
